ALS PCS: PCP MEDICAL DIRECTIVES
Source: Ontario ALS PCS v5.4 (April 2025), PCP Protocol Book 5.4, OBHG Companion Document (August 2026)
Scope: Primary Care Paramedic. "IV" routes require IV access (IV and Fluid Therapy is an auxiliary directive).

SCOPE BOUNDARY: This file is the complete PCP medication and procedure scope. Anything not listed here is outside PCP scope and must not appear in a generated scenario, not even as a withheld option.

1. Bronchoconstriction
- Salbutamol MDI: up to 800 mcg (8 puffs) at 25 kg or more, up to 600 mcg (6 puffs) under 25 kg; every 5-15 min PRN, max 3 doses
- Salbutamol NEB: 5 mg at 25 kg or more, 2.5 mg under 25 kg; every 5-15 min PRN, max 3 doses
- Epinephrine 1 mg/mL IM 0.01 mg/kg, max 0.5 mg, 1 dose: only with a history of asthma AND BVM ventilation required
- Dexamethasone 0.5 mg/kg PO/IM/IV, max 8 mg, 1 dose: history of asthma, COPD, or 20 pack-year smoking; not if already on PO or parenteral steroids

2. Hypoglycemia
- Oral glucose when alert and able to swallow safely (BLS PCS)
- Glucagon (altered LOA): 0.5 mg IM under 25 kg, 1 mg IM at 25 kg or more, or 3 mg IN at age 4 or older; every 20 min, max 2 doses; contraindicated in pheochromocytoma
- Dextrose IV (altered LOA, age 2 or older): D10W 0.2 g/kg (2 mL/kg) or D50W 0.5 g/kg (1 mL/kg), max 25 g per dose, every 10 min, max 2 doses

3. Cardiac Ischemia
- ASA 160-162 mg PO, 1 dose: age 18 or older, unaltered LOA, able to chew and swallow
  Contraindications: NSAID allergy or sensitivity, asthmatic with no prior ASA use, current active bleeding, CVA or TBI in the previous 24 hours
- Nitroglycerin 0.3 or 0.4 mg SL every 5 min: max 3 doses if STEMI, max 6 doses otherwise
  Conditions: age 18 or older, unaltered LOA, HR 60-159, SBP normotensive (100 or higher), prior history of nitroglycerin use OR IV access obtained
  Contraindications: nitrate allergy, phosphodiesterase inhibitor within 48 hours, SBP drop of one-third or more after nitroglycerin, 12-lead compatible with right ventricular MI

4. Acute Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema
- Nitroglycerin SL every 5 min, max 6 doses: 0.3 or 0.4 mg if SBP 100-139, 0.6 or 0.8 mg if SBP 140 or higher
  Conditions: age 18 or older, HR 60-159, SBP normotensive
  Contraindications: as for cardiac ischemia (no 12-lead needed before the first dose; acquire it as soon as possible)

5. Moderate to Severe Allergic Reaction
- Epinephrine 1 mg/mL IM 0.01 mg/kg, max 0.5 mg, minimum 5 min apart, max 2 doses: anaphylaxis only
- Diphenhydramine IM/IV, 1 dose: 25 mg at 25 to under 50 kg, 50 mg at 50 kg or more

6. Analgesia (age 12 or older, unaltered LOA)
- Acetaminophen PO, 1 dose: 960-1000 mg adult, 500-650 mg age 12 to under 18. Not within 4 hours of prior use, liver disease, vomiting, unable to tolerate oral, or suspected ischemic chest pain
- Ibuprofen 400 mg PO, 1 dose
- Ketorolac 10-15 mg IM/IV, 1 dose
  NSAID contraindications: NSAID within 6 hours, allergy, anticoagulation, active bleeding, peptic ulcer or GI bleed, pregnancy, asthma without prior NSAID use, CVA or TBI within 24 hours, renal impairment, suspected ischemic chest pain. Never give ibuprofen and ketorolac together.

7. Nausea / Vomiting (25 kg or more, unaltered LOA)
- Dimenhydrinate IV/IM: 25 mg at 25 to under 50 kg (1 dose); 25 or 50 mg at 50 kg or more, may repeat once after 30 min. Not in antihistamine, anticholinergic or tricyclic overdose, or with diphenhydramine
- Ondansetron 4 mg PO/IV/IM, 1 dose. Not with prolonged QT or apomorphine use. May be considered if dimenhydrinate gave no relief after 30 min

8. Opioid Toxicity and Withdrawal
- Naloxone (age 24 hours or older, altered LOA, RR under 10 or unable to ventilate adequately): 0.4 mg IV/IM, 0.8 mg SC or 2-4 mg IN, every 5 min, max 3 doses. Titrate to adequate breathing
- Buprenorphine/naloxone BUC/SL: 16 mg, then 8 mg every 10 min, cumulative max 24 mg. Only after naloxone this episode, age 16 or older, unaltered LOA, COWS 8 or more; not with methadone in the past 72 hours

9. Croup (age 6 months to under 8 years, URTI with barking cough)
- Dexamethasone 0.5 mg/kg PO, max 8 mg, 1 dose, any severity. Not if steroids in the past 48 hours or unable to tolerate oral
- Epinephrine 1 mg/mL NEB, 1 dose: 2.5 mg under 10 kg, 5 mg at 10 kg or more; stridor at rest and HR under 200

10. Suspected Adrenal Crisis
- Hydrocortisone 2 mg/kg IM/IV, max 100 mg, 1 dose: known primary adrenal failure, paramedics presented with the patient's vial, with hypoglycemia or GI symptoms, syncope, fever, altered LOA, tachycardia or hypotension

11. Medical Cardiac Arrest
- High-quality CPR, AED or manual defibrillation, rhythm analysis every 2 min, supraglottic airway when indicated
- Epinephrine 1 mg/mL IM 0.01 mg/kg, max 0.5 mg, 1 dose: only if anaphylaxis is suspected as the cause
- Medical TOR: arrest not witnessed by paramedics AND no ROSC after 20 min of resuscitation AND no defibrillation delivered. Patch early if extenuating circumstances
- No routine naloxone in confirmed arrest

12. Return of Spontaneous Circulation
- Titrate SpO2 to 94-98%, ETCO2 30-40 mmHg, avoid hyperventilation, 12-lead ECG
- Consider 0.9% NaCl bolus 10 mL/kg, max 1000 mL, if hypotensive with a clear chest

13. Supraglottic Airway
- For ventilatory assistance or airway control when other airway management is ineffective, with an absent gag reflex; max 2 attempts
- Contraindications: foreign body obstruction, known esophageal disease (varices), oropharyngeal trauma, caustic ingestion

14. Emergency Childbirth and Newborn Resuscitation
- Delivery, shoulder dystocia, breech delivery, prolapsed cord management, cord clamping and cutting
- Newborn: PPV if HR under 100; CPR if HR under 60 after 30 seconds of effective PPV

15. Home Dialysis Emergency Disconnect
- Clamp both the patient side and the machine side before disconnecting and capping

PCP AUXILIARY DIRECTIVES (the paramedic must be authorized for each)

16. Intravenous and Fluid Therapy
- IV access for actual or potential need for IV medication or fluid
- 0.9% NaCl bolus for hypotension (age 2 or older): 20 mL/kg, reassess every 100 mL (age 2-12) or 250 mL (12 or older), max 2000 mL. Not with fluid overload

17. Cardiogenic Shock
- STEMI-positive 12-lead with cardiogenic shock, age 18 or older, hypotension, clear chest: 0.9% NaCl 10 mL/kg IV, reassess every 250 mL, max 1000 mL

18. CPAP
- Age 18 or older, severe respiratory distress with signs of acute pulmonary edema or COPD, tachypnea, normotension, SpO2 under 90% or accessory muscle use
- Start 5 cmH2O, increase by 2.5 cmH2O every 5 min as needed, max 15 cmH2O; FiO2 50-100%
- Contraindications include asthma exacerbation, suspected pneumothorax, unprotected or unstable airway

19. Traumatic Hemorrhage
- Tranexamic acid 1000 mg IV/IM, 1 dose: age 16 or older, suspected traumatic hemorrhage with HR 110 or higher or hypotension
- Contraindications: more than 3 hours from injury, isolated head injury, allergy. Never delays transport or hemorrhage control

20. Tachydysrhythmia
- Modified Valsalva, max 2 attempts of 10-20 seconds: age 18 or older, unaltered LOA, HR 150 or higher, normotensive, narrow complex and regular
- Contraindications: sinus tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter

21. Lateral Patellar Dislocation
- Reduction, max 2 attempts: age 10-50, unaltered LOA; not after high velocity or direct knee trauma

22. Seizure
- Treat and discharge criteria only. There is no PCP seizure medication.

NOT DIRECTIVES BUT ALWAYS IN PLAY
- Stroke: apply the Ontario Acute Stroke Protocol and regional bypass criteria; last known well time drives destination; confirm BGL
- DNR: confirm and honour a valid DNR confirmation form
